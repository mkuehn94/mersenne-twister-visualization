Understanding the Mersenne Twister Algorithm

Understanding one of the world's most popular pseudorandom number generators. Build your own, reverse engineer one by input numbers all static in your browser.

This equation basically is the whole generator. It holds a state array of 624 numbers. Generation starts with the first bit of k attaches it onto the low 31 bits of number k+1, shifts and scrambles that a bit (represented by matrix A) and XORs the result with number k+397. Then the number gets one more scramble T (called tempting) and out comes zk.

A important parameter that measures when sequences repeat. It's 219937 − so basically impossible (SHA-256 collison never happened and that's only 2^256)

In linear algebra look up the term periodic matrix for a deeper understanding

Notation

The math can look quite complex, while they are really not. Almost everything is just bit operations you'd write in C as ^, &, >>.

A lot are statistic parameters/metrics you can just google

symbolmeaningexample
XOR, a ^ b in C. one of the two bits is 1 -> 1 else 0
AND / OR, a & b and a | b. requiring exclusive or simultanious 1 for both bits
right/left shift by k bits. padded with zeros.
upper bits of x appended to the lower bits of y. For MT19937: the top 1 bit of x plus the low 31 bits of y.
wrapping around, exactly like a uint32_t overflowing. or modulo 2^32
probably the most important operator to understand a field with just 0 and 1, where + is XOR and × is AND. Every MT operation (XOR, shift, AND with a fixed mask) is linear over F2, so the whole generator becomes a giant matrix. That's the trick behind basically everything about MT.
number of bits (Hamming weight). shows up in the zero-excess plot.
the polynomial of the big matrix, with bits as coefficients. the degree is the size of the recurrence, and if it's irreducible and 2deg−1 is prime you get the full period.
Berlekamp–Massey, an algorithm that consumes a bit sequence and finds the shortest linear recurrence (LFSR) that gets produced by it. its length L is the linear complexity. truly random bits give L ≈ n/2, MT19937 gets stuck at 19937.
tempering and its inverse (MT exclusive algorithm). T only mixes bits inside one word, and it can be undone exactly. demonstrated in the weak spots section.
k-distribution (also very important for RNGs): the largest possible k so that every pattern of the top v bits of k consecutive outputs shows up equally often over the full period. higher = better, ⌊p/v⌋ would be the maximum.
chi-squared: distance of the histogram counts from expectation. the p-value is the chance that a perfectly random source looks at least this bad. if p becomes tiny i.e(p < 0.001) means there is a bias somewhere.
autocorrelation is there some correlation between the current number and a number generated h steps later? for a good generator every rh should obviously be close to 0 and about 95% of the bars stay inside the band ±1.96/√N. bars sticking way out = correlation is present between numbers that should be indipendant.
Monte Carlo π: Imagine a circle in a square. coordinates are drawn from the generator. it measures how uniformly the distribution is by estimating PI. at infinite samples drawn and perfect uniform distribution we would estimate PI perfectly.
Just a normal distribution. careful, same notation φ as the polynomial. Normals are defined by Mean and standard deviation.

Why 19937?

thats not just a fun fact to know where the name comes from but essential to the lack of reoccurring numbers. the period of a matrix recurrence comes from its characteristic polynomial .Matsumoto and Nishimura picked so that is a prime. then you only need to check that is irreducible and you never have to factor a 6002-digit number. that's why it's called MT19937.

Linear complexity profile

L(n): the shortest linear recurrence that explains the first n output bits

for the first ~39 874 bits MT looks exactly like coin flips. then the line just goes flat, because the bits follow a recurrence of degree 19 937 and Berlekamp–Massey has it figured out. that's why MT fails TestU01's linear complexity tests (a short LCG would fail them way earlier).

Tempering and equidistribution

the raw words already have the full period, so why scramble them again? because the raw top bits aren't spread out nicely when you look at several outputs at once. tempering is a fixed, invertible matrix that shuffles bits inside each word to fix exactly that.

k-distribution to v-bit accuracy

k(v) for a 607-bit twister, same twist with and without tempering

this is computed exactly, no sampling: k(v) is the rank of a big GF(2) matrix whose rows are "bit j of output t" and whose columns are the 607 state bits. without tempering it already misses the bound from v = 2 on. put MT19937's masks in and most of that gap disappears. pretty convincing argument for those weird constants.

Weak spots

MT was made for simulations and it's great at that. it was never supposed to be used for anything secret though, and here's why: everything it does is linear and invertible, so 624 outputs give away the whole state. and then you can even walk it back to the seed.

Attack: clone a generator from its own output

say someone runs MT19937 with a secret seed and has already burned through an unknown number of outputs. all you get to see is the numbers it hands out. turns out that's enough to predict every number it will ever produce. watch:

1. Observe
grab 624 outputs in a row: .
2. Untemper each one
tempering undoes cleanly: hands back the hidden state word behind every output.
3. Predict
624 state words in a row are the complete state. load them into a fresh MT and it has the same future as the victim.
4. Rewind to the seed
the twist runs backwards too. walk back until the words look freshly seeded, invert the seeding, and and drop out.

Zero-excess: it takes forever to recover from a sparse state

start with a state that has exactly one 1-bit. each new word only depends on three old ones, so the ones spread really slowly. it takes hundreds of thousands of outputs before the state looks random again (half the bits set, ρ ≈ ½). WELL (2006) was built to fix exactly this.
